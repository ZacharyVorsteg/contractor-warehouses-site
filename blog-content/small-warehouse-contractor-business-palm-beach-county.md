## Who Uses Small Contractor Warehouse Space

The trades most commonly searching for small warehouse space in Palm Beach County fall into a few clear categories.

**HVAC contractors** need dry, accessible storage for refrigerant, coils, sheet metal stock, and staging room for commercial units before delivery. A single commercial install can require staging $10,000–$20,000 of equipment in advance, and running that out of a residential garage creates both operational and HOA problems.

**Plumbing contractors** stock pipe lengths, fixtures, PEX fittings, and water heaters that degrade quickly when left in unshaded Florida heat. A covered bay extends the life of inventory and makes morning job departures faster.

**Electrical contractors** deal with conduit runs, wire reels, panels, and breakers that need organized racking. Most 1- to 3-person electrical shops operate effectively out of 600–800 square feet with proper shelving and no wasted motion.

**Roofing and landscaping contractors** typically need less enclosed space but more yard area or covered outdoor storage for material staging, trailers, and equipment that cannot sit in a residential driveway overnight.

**General contractors and remodelers** tend to need the most versatile configuration: a bay tall enough for a lift, a small office corner for takeoffs and contracts, and clear staging for job kits.

If you are at the point where your truck doubles as your warehouse, it is worth reviewing the [signs your contractor business has outgrown the home garage](https://warehousesforcontractors.com/blog/outgrown-home-garage-contractor-warehouse/).

## What "Small" Actually Means for a Contractor

In commercial real estate, "small" industrial space generally means 500 to 3,000 square feet. For most single-trade contractors running 2–6 employees, the practical working range is 800–1,500 square feet. According to WareSpace (2026), a national small-bay industrial operator, contractors most commonly start their first lease in the 800–1,200 square foot range before moving into larger bays as crew size and revenue increase. ([warespace.com](https://warespace.com/))

Here is a practical sizing guide based on trade type:

| Contractor Type | Recommended Min Sq Ft | Key Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1-person electrical or plumbing | 600–800 sq ft | Racking, single vehicle bay |
| 3–5 person HVAC crew | 1,000–1,500 sq ft | Staging area, refrigerant storage, tall bay |
| Landscaping / irrigation | 800–1,200 sq ft | Covered yard access, equipment storage |
| Small GC / remodeler | 1,200–2,000 sq ft | Multi-use bay, small office area |
| Roofing contractor | 1,500–2,500 sq ft | Material staging, trailer parking |

The real test is not square footage on paper—it is whether your crew can prep a full job without reshuffling everything they organized the day before.

## Understanding NNN: Your Real Monthly Cost

Most industrial leases in South Florida are structured as NNN (triple net) leases. According to CARR Commercial Real Estate (2026), a national tenant-representation firm, NNN charges pass three categories of building operating expenses directly to the tenant: property taxes, building insurance, and common area maintenance (CAM). ([NNN lease guide](https://carr.us/blog/what-does-nnn-lease-triple-net-lease-mean/))

According to Matthew Antonis CRE (2026), NNN costs on industrial properties typically range from $4 to $12 per square foot annually, depending on location, property age, and local tax assessments. For Palm Beach County small-bay space, $5–$8/sqft is a reasonable working estimate. ([NNN lease breakdown](https://www.mantoniscre.com/blog/understanding-the-triple-net-lease-a-guide-for-investors-and-tenants))

Using $6/sqft NNN on a 1,000 sq ft space:
- Base rent: $15.32/sqft × 1,000 = $1,277/month
- NNN charges: $6/sqft × 1,000 ÷ 12 = $500/month
- **Total estimated monthly cost: ~$1,777**

## Traditional Lease vs. Flex and Shared Space

Not every contractor needs a 3-to-5 year traditional industrial lease from day one. Smaller operators or businesses in an early growth phase sometimes find better economics in flex or co-warehousing arrangements.

| Option | Typical Sq Ft Range | Est. Monthly All-In | Lease Term | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional NNN industrial | 1,000–5,000+ | $1,500–$5,000+ | 3–5 years | Established trades with stable crew size |
| Small-bay flex park | 500–2,000 | $1,200–$3,500 | 1–3 years | Growing contractors, lower upfront cost |
| Co-warehouse / shared bay | 200–800 | $600–$1,500 | Month-to-month | Solo operators, startup phase |
| Self-storage (climate) | 150–300 | $200–$500 | Month-to-month | Overflow inventory only—not a working bay |

Self-storage is worth addressing specifically because many contractors default to it when they outgrow the van. It is not a substitute for warehouse space. Most self-storage facilities prohibit vehicle drive-in, freight delivery, any working activity, and storage of regulated materials such as refrigerant or certain chemicals. It serves as overflow storage only and creates compliance exposure if used as a primary business address.

## Palm Beach County Submarkets for Contractor Space

Palm Beach County's industrial base is concentrated in established corridors. Submarket pricing and availability vary, so knowing where to search matters.

**Riviera Beach / Port of Palm Beach area** holds a significant concentration of older but functional multi-tenant industrial product. Pricing along Australian Avenue and Blue Heron Boulevard tends to run at or slightly below county average, making it one of the most active submarkets for small-bay contractor space.

**Boynton Beach / Delray Beach** is seeing continued activity at the institutional level. According to Berger Commercial Realty Corp. **Jupiter / Palm Beach Gardens** prices higher due to land costs and newer product, but makes geographic sense for contractors whose client concentration is in the northern county.

## What to Look for in a Contractor Warehouse

Not all small industrial space is suited to trade contractor operations. Before signing a lease, physically walk the space and verify:

**Grade-level drive-in access.** Most small contractor operations need grade-level doors, not elevated loading docks. Confirm door width accommodates your largest vehicle and overhead clearance is sufficient for a van, box truck, or trailer.

**Electrical service.** A standard small industrial bay often provides 100–200 amp service. HVAC contractors testing equipment may need 3-phase power. Confirm actual service before you sign and budget for any upgrade the landlord will or will not fund.

**Ceiling height.** Even in a smaller bay, ceiling height determines how much you can rack and how efficiently you can organize. A 14-foot clear minimum is a practical floor for contractor use; 16–18 feet opens up real vertical storage options.

**Zoning confirmation.** Confirm the space is zoned for light industrial or general industrial use. Some flex parks restrict vehicle storage, spray equipment, chemical storage, or signage in ways that directly conflict with how contractors operate.

## Loading Access and Vehicle Clearance

This is often the detail that eliminates an otherwise attractive space. A 1,200 sq ft bay in a multi-tenant park can look perfect on paper but have a shared drive aisle that cannot accommodate a 40-foot trailer turning radius. Walk the property with your largest vehicle in mind before you request a lease.

Also ask about access hours. Many multi-tenant industrial parks limit access to standard business hours. If your crew departs job sites at 5:30 AM, a park that gates access at 8 PM is a functional problem regardless of the rent.

## Power, Lighting, and HVAC Requirements

Florida's climate creates demands that contractors in other states do not face. An uncooled warehouse bay in Palm Beach County in July reaches working temperatures that top 100°F before noon. For crew prepping jobs, sorting materials, or performing light fabrication, a space with ventilation—at minimum a wall unit or industrial fan—is not a luxury.

Confirm whether power is individually metered or included in CAM. Some small-bay parks meter each tenant; others bundle it into operating expenses. Know which structure you are agreeing to before you sign, and ask for prior-tenant utility bills if available.

## Lease Term: How Long Should You Commit?

A common mistake is over-committing early (a 5-year lease when the business may need more space in 18 months) or under-committing at a higher monthly premium. As a practical guide:

**Under 2 years in business:** favor flexibility. A flex park or co-warehouse on a 12-month term gives you an exit if the business grows faster or slower than planned. The monthly premium is real but so is the optionality.

**2–5 years in business with a stable crew:** a 3-year NNN lease typically delivers the best rent-per-square-foot and locks in predictable monthly costs.

**5+ years with 6 or more employees:** a 5-year lease with renewal options may make sense to protect your location and stabilize occupancy cost as a known line item in your overhead.

## When to Move Up from Your Current Space

The signal is usually operational: job prep takes 30 minutes or more because materials are buried, crew members work in each other's way, or you are routinely leaving overflow inventory in a vehicle overnight. A space that costs your crew time every single morning is costing you more than a larger lease would.

If you have 3 or more employees and are generating $800,000 or more in annual revenue, you are likely undersized at 800 square feet and ready for 1,200–1,800 square feet. Do not wait for the space to become a crisis—availability in the submarkets most suited to contractor use can thin out quickly, and the better parks fill from referral before they list publicly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does small warehouse space for a contractor cost in Palm Beach County?**
At Q2 2026 market rates of $15.32 per square foot (Berger Commercial Realty Corp. / CoStar Group), a 1,000 sq ft bay runs approximately $1,277 per month in base rent. Add NNN charges of $4–$8/sqft annually and total monthly occupancy cost for a well-located 1,000 sq ft bay typically falls between $1,600 and $2,100.

**Do I need a commercial address to hold a Florida contractor license?**
Florida's Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) does not require a commercial address to obtain or renew a state contractor license. However, local zoning ordinances in residential areas generally prohibit using a home as a business operations center with employee parking, fleet vehicles, or material storage. Verify local zoning compliance before treating a residential address as your business base.

**What is the minimum lease term for industrial space in Palm Beach County?**
Most traditional NNN industrial leases in the county run 3–5 years. Small-bay flex parks and co-warehousing operations often offer 12-month terms or month-to-month at a premium. Short-term industrial leases at traditional NNN pricing are available but uncommon—typically found in older multi-tenant parks with higher vacancy.

**Can I use a self-storage unit as contractor warehouse space?**
Self-storage units are appropriate for overflow inventory only. They typically prohibit vehicle access inside the unit, freight delivery, any work activity, and storage of certain regulated materials including refrigerant. Using a self-storage unit as a primary working base creates compliance exposure and is not a functional substitute for a contractor bay.

**How long does it take to lease small warehouse space in Palm Beach County?**
From initial search to keys in hand, a straightforward small-bay lease typically takes 30–60 days: 1–2 weeks to identify and tour available spaces, 1–2 weeks for lease negotiation and legal review, and 1–2 weeks for utilities transfer, security deposit, and move-in prep.
